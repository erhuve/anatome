# Anatomeme curriculum and learning research

Researched 30 October 2026. This is a content plan and original factual notes, not a complete course or an anatomist's validation of any 3D mesh.

## Curriculum basis

[OpenStax A&P 2e preface](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/preface) describes a sequence aligned to most two-semester college courses. Use that sequence as a transparent baseline, with instructor-specific practical lists layered on top. Full A&P requires mechanisms and regulation as well as structure recognition; a skeletal atlas alone is not complete A&P.

[HAPS Learning Outcomes](https://www.hapsweb.org/haps-learning-outcomes/) is an authoritative cross-check for undergraduate breadth. HAPS explicitly says its outcomes exceed what one course can cover and instructors must select. The [2019 A&P outcomes PDF](https://www.hapsweb.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/AP-Learning-Outcomes-PDF.pdf) covers anatomical reference, histology, skeletal identification, muscular function, homeostasis, and organ systems. It is a reference, not a universally required memorization checklist. Describe the app as “aligned with a typical college A&P sequence,” not “everything your exam requires.”

## Proposed course map

These are product recommendations informed by the curriculum sources; they are not copied learning-outcome language.

| Study block | Structure / identification | Physiological connection |
|---|---|---|
| Foundations | Anatomical position, subject-right/left, planes, regions, cavities | Homeostatic feedback, organization, structure–function relationships |
| Cells and tissues | Cell structures; epithelial, connective, muscle and nervous tissue | Transport, cell communication, tissue repair |
| Integument | Skin layers, glands, hair, nails | Barrier, temperature control, sensation |
| Skeletal system | Bone tissue, axial and appendicular bones, landmarks | Remodeling, mineral balance, leverage |
| Joints | Joint types and structures, movement terminology | Stability and range of motion |
| Muscular system | Regional muscles, attachments, actions; muscle histology | Excitation–contraction coupling, force, energy |
| Nervous system and senses | CNS/PNS, pathways, cranial nerves, sensory structures | Electrical signaling, reflexes, sensation |
| Endocrine | Glands, hormones and targets | Feedback and metabolic control |
| Cardiovascular / lymphatic / immune | Heart, vessels, blood cells and lymphatic structures | Pressure, flow, exchange and defense |
| Respiratory / digestive / urinary | Organ pathways and microanatomy | Gas exchange, absorption, filtration, fluid/acid–base regulation |
| Reproduction / development | Reproductive organs, gametes, early development | Endocrine regulation and development |

Only mark a block “available” when its study material and assessment are implemented. A roadmap item must never increase a learner's completed-content total.

## First playable lab: appendicular bones

Recommended initial validated slice: **femur + humerus**, followed by scapula, tibia and paired forearm bones. Prioritize correct geometry, landmark registration and orientation over a procedurally impressive full skeleton.

### Femur facts / landmark list

Study: head, fovea capitis, neck, greater trochanter, lesser trochanter, intertrochanteric line (anterior), intertrochanteric crest (posterior), gluteal tuberosity, linea aspera (posterior shaft), medial/lateral condyles, medial/lateral epicondyles, adductor tubercle, intercondylar fossa (posterior distal), patellar surface (anterior distal). The head articulates with the acetabulum; the condyles articulate with the tibia; the patella articulates with the patellar surface. Head is medial; greater trochanter is lateral; linea aspera and intercondylar fossa establish the posterior surface. To determine side, orient proximal end upward, place posterior features posteriorly, then locate the medially directed head. A screen-left label is insufficient because the camera can rotate. [OpenStax 8.4](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-4-bones-of-the-lower-limb)

### Humerus facts / landmark list

Study: head, anatomical neck, surgical neck, greater tubercle, lesser tubercle, intertubercular groove, deltoid tuberosity, medial/lateral epicondyles, capitulum, trochlea, radial fossa, coronoid fossa, olecranon fossa. Head faces medially and articulates with the scapula's glenoid cavity. Lesser tubercle is anterior; greater tubercle is lateral. Trochlea is medial and articulates with ulna; capitulum is lateral and articulates with radius. Olecranon fossa is posterior; radial/coronoid fossae are anterior. Laterality task: put the head superiorly, turn olecranon fossa posteriorly, and use medial head plus distal joint landmarks to determine side. [OpenStax 8.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-2-bones-of-the-upper-limb)

### Scapula expansion

Study: spine, acromion, coracoid process, glenoid cavity, supraspinous/infraspinous/subscapular fossae, superior/medial/lateral borders, superior/inferior angles, suprascapular notch, supra-/infraglenoid tubercles. Laterality uses the spine posteriorly and glenoid laterally; the inferior angle points inferiorly. [OpenStax 8.1](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/8-1-the-pectoral-girdle)

### Orientation requirements

Right and left always belong to the specimen/person. Camera orientation changes the view, not anatomical identity. Anatomical position and directional terms remain the reference regardless of the pose. Teach this explicitly before left/right practice. Preset buttons should state what surface is being viewed and have checked transforms. Randomization must rotate the camera or specimen rigidly; it must not mirror the same mesh and silently relabel its side. [OpenStax 1.6](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/1-6-anatomical-terminology)

## Parallel tissue and muscle tracks

### Tissue seed scope

Start with original study descriptions plus separately licensed real microscopy, rather than fabricated “histology” textures. In epithelial examples, grade naming by layers and apical cell shape, and ask learners to connect organization with location/function. Core initial examples: simple squamous, simple cuboidal, simple columnar, pseudostratified ciliated columnar, stratified squamous, transitional. Teach basement membrane and apical/basal orientation. [OpenStax 4.2](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-2-epithelial-tissue)

Add loose areolar, adipose, reticular, dense regular/irregular connective tissue, cartilage, bone and blood as coverage grows. Connective classification depends on cells and extracellular matrix, including fiber organization. [OpenStax chapter 4 review](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-chapter-review)

Contrast skeletal muscle (striated long fibers, peripheral nuclei), cardiac muscle (striated, branching, central nuclei, intercalated discs), and smooth muscle (spindle-shaped, central nuclei, no evident striations). Require multiple fields/exemplars before claiming tissue recognition mastery. [OpenStax 4.4](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/4-4-muscle-tissue-and-motion)

### Muscle seed scope

Upper-limb start: deltoid, biceps brachii, brachialis, triceps brachii, pectoralis major, latissimus dorsi, and four rotator-cuff muscles. Record identity, compartment/depth, origin, insertion, action, and optional innervation. Keep head-/part-specific actions distinct. Good linked tasks: locate deltoid tuberosity then name attaching muscle; identify biceps then recall elbow flexion and supination. [OpenStax 11.5](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-5-muscles-of-the-pectoral-girdle-and-upper-limbs)

Lower-limb start: gluteus maximus/medius, iliopsoas, quadriceps members, hamstring members, sartorius, adductors, tibialis anterior, gastrocnemius and soleus. Link compartments, joint crossing and movement rather than teach all actions as undifferentiated word lists. [OpenStax 11.6](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology-2e/pages/11-6-appendicular-muscles-of-the-pelvic-girdle-and-lower-limbs)

## Learning design

1. **Orient → inspect → retrieve → explain → transfer.** Guided exploration introduces a few landmarks. Hide the names, ask learners to point/type, reveal corrective feedback, then revisit from another view. Separate name recall, spatial identification and laterality in progress; confidence alone is not achievement.
2. **Retrieval is a learning activity.** Roediger & Karpicke's primary experiments found better delayed retention after retrieval than repeated study. Product implication: offer short low-stakes identification practicals and an answer attempt before revealing. The study used prose; applying the principle to 3D bone tasks is a reasoned design inference. [Original study, 2006](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/psychological-science/j.1467-9280.2006.01693.x/)
3. **Compare confusable examples.** Kornell & Bjork found benefits from interleaving category exemplars. After initial guided familiarity, mix similar bones, distal landmarks and tissue classes; ask for the discriminating feature. This is transfer from a visual-category study, not direct proof for every anatomy exercise. [Original paper](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/07/Kornell_Bjork_2008_PsychScience.pdf)
4. **Space revisits without making a deck the app.** Cepeda et al. tested fact learning across gaps and delayed retention; effective spacing depended on the intended retention interval. Use “revisit weak structures” as a secondary session option, not a claimed universal optimal algorithm. A simple declared schedule is acceptable, but call it a heuristic. [Primary study and manuscript](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/0kp5q19x)
5. **Practical readiness must transfer.** Mix practiced and unpracticed views, isolated and in-context structures, and multiple real specimens. Do not equate repeated success on one highlighted mesh with readiness for a physical lab practical. This is a product recommendation, not a validated readiness threshold.

## Content and asset record

Every structure should have: stable ID; accepted term + aliases; system/region; prerequisite concepts; original short description; source URL/section; validated landmark coordinates tied to model version; side; visible views; question types; and review status. Every asset needs creator, source, license/version, permitted uses, attribution, verified handedness, units/orientation, and accuracy review.

Keep terminology/data separate from rendering. A structurally accurate dataset must survive replacing a model. Model-space anchors must be reviewed on the exact mesh before enabling click-to-identify grading.
